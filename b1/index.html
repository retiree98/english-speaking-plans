The idea

Speaking improves by speaking, out loud, every day

At B1 you can already talk about familiar things: yourself, your family, your work. The next step, B2, is speaking longer, more freely and without stopping to search for words, on more topics. That comes from practice, so both plans spend most of the time on your voice: copying speakers, recording yourself, and talking with people and AI.

The General plan builds the English of daily life: family, food, shopping, health, travel, past experiences, plans and simple opinions.

The Quran plan uses the material you already love: prophets' stories told in simple English for children, tajweed explained in English, and the meanings of the surahs you teach. About a third of the time (conversations, AI chats) stays on everyday English, so daily life improves too.

As an Arabic speaker you already have some English sounds (like ث in think), and the others can be learned by comparing them with Arabic letters you know. The pronunciation section shows how.

You also have a rare advantage. Tajweed trained your ear and mouth to notice exact articulation points (مخارج). This plan uses that skill for English sounds.

Why this works

  • A balanced diet of English. Paul Nation's "four strands" model gives roughly equal time to listening, speaking, studying phrases and fluency practice. The daily routine follows that balance.
  • Listening you can understand. At B1, most new words come from listening to English that is slightly above your level, so the sources here are slower, clear English made for learners and for children.
  • Shadowing (speaking along with a recording) improves listening and the rhythm of speech.
  • Repeating the same task makes speech smoother and more accurate each time. That is why you record, listen and record again, and why the 3/2/1 drill repeats one talk three times.
  • Spaced flashcards are one of the strongest findings in learning research: reviewing just before you forget makes words stick.
  • Clear before perfect. Research on being understood puts consonants, vowel length and word stress first, so the sounds come in that order.

Realistic expectations. Cambridge estimates about 200 hours of guided learning to move up one level. These 12 weeks give about 60 focused hours: enough to feel much more confident, speak for longer and be understood more easily. Reaching a full B2 usually takes 6–9 months, so after week 12 you repeat the cycle with harder material.

Week 0 · one evening

Set up and record your starting point

Every day · about 50 minutes, plus easy listening

Daily routine

Blocks 1 and 3 are the same every day. Block 2 changes by day (see the weekly rhythm). The easy listening fits into time you already have.

15 min

Listen and shadow

Play 1 minute of an ELLLO B1 or VOA Learning English recording (both have transcripts). Listen once, read along once, then speak with the speaker, copying their rhythm and melody. Repeat 3–4 times.

Play 1 minute of this week's prophet story for children (Omar & Hana or Zaky, with English captions on; each week card below has a direct search for that week's story). Children's stories use clear, slower English: perfect at B1, and it is exactly the English your students understand. Listen once, read the captions once, then speak with the narrator. Repeat 3–4 times.

25 min

Today's speaking focus

The day's task from the weekly rhythm. Always out loud, and usually recorded.

10 min

Phrases and review

Add 5 useful phrases from today to your notebook and Anki, plus the correct version of any mistake you made. Review old cards. Say each phrase aloud in a full sentence, not just the word.

Add 5 phrases from today to Anki, the correct version of any mistake you made, and the English meaning of 1–2 verses from a short surah you teach (from Quran.com). Say each one aloud in a full sentence.

20–30 min

Easy listening, anytime

While cooking, walking or travelling, listen to something you understand without effort: VOA Learning English stories, ELLLO conversations, or 6 Minute English from the BBC once those feel easy. No notes, no pressure. This is where new words quietly come from.

While cooking, walking or travelling, listen to more prophet stories for children (Omar & Hana, Zaky). From phase 3, try Mufti Menk's Stories of the Prophets, which is for adults. No notes, no pressure. This is where new words quietly come from.

The two rules that keep you going. On a busy or tired day, do the 10-minute minimum: one shadowing round and one recording. It still counts. And never miss two days in a row: one missed day is normal; two in a row is how plans stop.

All day, free: think in English while cooking or walking. Describe what you are doing in your head ("I'm going to call the clinic and make an appointment"). When a word is missing, note it and look it up later.

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–4

Clear sounds, daily habit

Goal: speak every day without stress, talk for 1 minute about yourself without stopping, and fix the sounds that most confuse listeners.

Phase 2 · Weeks 5–8

Longer answers, more topics

Goal: speak for 2 minutes on a familiar topic, tell a story in the past, link ideas with because, so, but, then, and hold two real conversations every week.

Phase 3 · Weeks 9–12

Toward B2: opinions and freedom

Goal: give and explain your opinion, solve problems in conversation, and talk for 3 minutes on an everyday topic from short notes.

Goal: explain tajweed rules simply, answer parents' questions, and teach a 5-minute lesson in English from short notes.

Pronunciation for Arabic speakers

English sounds for Arabic speakers

Use your tajweed ear

Most English sounds are close to an Arabic letter you already say every day. Start from that letter, then change one small thing.

You already know where each Arabic letter comes from in the mouth. Many English sounds map neatly onto that knowledge.

/θ/ think, /ð/ thisSame place as ث and ذ. You already have these sounds; just use them.think / sink, three / tree, this / dis

Check any word's stress and sound in the Cambridge Dictionary (UK and US audio), and hear it in real sentences on YouGlish. The aim is to be clear, not to copy one accent; British and American sources are both fine.

Everyday English

Daily-life phrases

Useful phrases for the situations in the 12-week map. Say each one aloud, then change it to fit your own life.

Small talk

  • How are you? How was your weekend?
  • I've been quite busy this week.
  • What do you like doing in your free time?
  • Oh, that sounds nice!
  • Sorry, can you say that again, please?
  • Sorry, what does … mean?

Appointments and calls

  • Hello, I'd like to make an appointment, please.
  • Is Tuesday morning possible?
  • Can you spell that for me, please?
  • Can you speak a bit more slowly, please?
  • Thank you. See you on Tuesday.

Shops and problems

  • Excuse me, do you have this in a smaller size?
  • How much is this, please?
  • I bought this last week, but it doesn't work.
  • Can I change it, please?

Telling and giving opinions

  • First…, then…, after that…, in the end…
  • I think… because…
  • In my opinion…
  • I agree. / I'm not sure I agree, because…
  • For example…

Speaking practice · both plans

Three ways to get speaking time

1 · Record yourself

The most important habit. Speak for 1 minute, listen back, note 3 things to improve, then record again. Any mistake you hear more than once goes on your top-mistakes list. Compare recordings month by month.

2 · Talk with people, twice a week

Real conversation is where speaking grows fastest: 30 minutes on Wednesday and 15 minutes on Monday. Language exchange on Tandem or HelloTalk (free tiers): you teach Arabic, they help with English. Or a sister, friend or colleague who speaks English.

3 · Talk with AI

Free voice chat in the ChatGPT or Gemini app, as extra practice or when no one is free. AI is patient, so it is a safe place to try new words. Free tiers have daily limits, so 15 minutes is the right size. Use the prompt below.

AI speaking partner prompt

You are my English speaking partner. I am a B1 learner and I want to speak more fluently in everyday life. Today we will talk about: [TOPIC]. Rules: - Speak a little slowly and use simple, common words. If I don't understand, say it again in a different way. - Keep each of your turns short (1–2 sentences) and always end with a question, so I do most of the talking. - After every 5 of my answers, stop and give me: 3 mistakes I made with the correct version, 2 better ways to say something I said, and 1 useful phrase for my topic. - If I stop for a long time, help me with the first words of a sentence.You are my English speaking partner. If I don't understand, say it again in a different way. - Keep each of your turns short (1–2 sentences) and always end with a question, so I do most of the talking. - After every 5 of my answers, stop and give me: 3 mistakes I made with the correct version, 2 better ways to say something I said, and 1 useful phrase for my topic. - If I stop for a long time, help me with the first words of a sentence.

Topic ideas: your family · your favourite food and how to cook it · your neighbourhood · a trip you remember · your plans for next year · what you do at the weekend · a problem with something you bought.

Topic ideas: this week's prophet story · how you teach a new letter · your students · a normal day at work · what you like about teaching · how parents can help at home.

Staying safe with strangers online: choose female partners, keep chats inside the app, don't share your phone number, address or photos, and leave any chat that feels uncomfortable. A sister, friend or colleague who speaks English is always a good first partner.
